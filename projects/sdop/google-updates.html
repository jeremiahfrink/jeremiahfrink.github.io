Shipped in July and August

Google announced a great deal more than this. Most of it needs Education Plus, the Teaching and Learning add-on, or Google AI Pro, none of which we have. These five are ours.

AugustGemini Notebook

Share a notebook so someone takes their own copy

Build once, then everyone gets their own version to grow. The most useful thing on this page.

Sharing used to mean everyone worked inside yours. Now a person takes a copy: your sources come along, and everything they add afterward is theirs alone. It does not stay in sync, which is the whole point.

For you as a coach

Your own work

  • A new teacher orientation notebook, copied to every new hire, instead of the same forty minute conversation eleven times.
  • Your working notebook copied to a co-coach, so you stop maintaining parallel versions of the same thing.
  • A notebook per building with its schedules, staff and quirks. Copy the shell when you pick up a new site.

For the teacher

Design, develop, manage

  • Build the leveled set once against the text a whole grade already teaches, then hand every teacher a copy to add their own material on top.
  • A department copies one unit notebook and each teacher tunes it to their own sections without touching anyone else's.
  • The teacher taking over a course mid year gets a copy rather than a box of files.
  • A co-teaching pair each copies the planning notebook, then compares what they each added.

With students

Grades 9 to 12 direct

  • Seed it with the unit sources and nothing else. Students copy and build their own flashcards, quizzes and summaries. Same content, their construction.
  • A research project where everyone starts from the same vetted sources, so the argument is about thinking rather than who found a better website.
  • Each class period gets its own copy and the notebooks diverge by what each group asks. Compare them at the end.
  • K to 8: the teacher builds the set and students choose which format they use.
AugustGoogle SlidesGoogle Vids

Record a presentation straight from Slides

Narrate over a deck, edit by transcript. Not AI, which matters for some of your teachers.

Record narration over a deck you already have and get a video out. You edit by cutting the transcript rather than dragging a timeline. No new tool, no new file to manage.

For you as a coach

Your own work

  • The thing you explain in every building. Record it, send the link, stop scheduling the fifth meeting.
  • Record your own session so people who could not attend are not permanently behind.
  • A two minute walkthrough attached to any resource you send, so it arrives with its own explanation.
  • Your update for a principal, recorded, so they watch it when they have four minutes rather than never.

For the teacher

Design, develop, manage

  • The ten minute introduction delivered four times a day, and again for every absent student. Record once.
  • The procedure everyone forgets in October, recorded in September and posted where they can find it.
  • The back to school night presentation, recorded for families who could not come.
  • Sub plans that are actually usable, because the substitute hears the teacher explain the lesson.

With students

All grades

  • Students narrate their own deck, so the assessment is what they can explain rather than what they arranged on a slide.
  • A student who freezes in front of a room records instead, and still has to do the thinking.
  • Group projects where each member records their own section, which makes contribution visible.
  • Students record an explanation in September and again in May against the same prompt.
AugustGoogle Drive

Comment on a video at the moment it applies

Feedback stops being a paragraph at the bottom.

Timestamped comments on video in Drive. The note sits at the second it is about, so the reader lands on the moment instead of hunting for it.

For you as a coach

Your own work

  • Record a lesson with permission, then comment together at the three moments worth discussing. The video keeps you both looking at the same thing.
  • Annotate a model lesson at each move, so a teacher sees why and not just what.
  • Leave observation notes inside the video rather than in a document nobody opens twice.
  • Two coaches comment on the same clip independently, then compare what each of you noticed.

For the teacher

Design, develop, manage

  • Feedback on a student presentation at the exact moment the argument falls apart.
  • A teacher records themselves once a marking period and comments on their own video before anyone else sees it.
  • Grade a performance task without rewatching the whole thing to find the moment again.

With students

All grades

  • Peer review at timestamps, which forces students to point at something specific instead of writing "good job."
  • A student comments on their own recorded work before the teacher does. Then compare the two sets.
  • Music, theatre and PE: comment on the run rather than describing it afterward from memory.
  • A class annotates one shared video together as a close reading exercise.
AugustWorkspace StudioGemini Notebook

Have a notebook feed itself

Point a flow at a folder and stop re-uploading.

A Workspace Studio flow adds new files to a notebook automatically. Set it up once against a folder and anything dropped in from then on is already there.

For you as a coach

Your own work

  • A folder per curriculum you support, feeding a notebook you can question before any visit.
  • One notebook holding all district guidance, fed automatically, so you always answer from the current version.
  • Your own capture folder: screenshots, notes, articles, session materials. Searchable without you organizing it.
  • A folder where you drop every question a teacher asks you. By December you can see what the district actually needs.

For the teacher

Design, develop, manage

  • A unit folder feeds the class notebook, so materials added mid unit appear without anyone maintaining it.
  • A running notebook of everything a course covered, which is worth a great deal in April.
  • A grade team folder where anyone can contribute and the shared notebook stays current.

With students

Grades 9 to 12 direct

  • Students submit research sources to a folder and the class notebook grows into a shared collection they can all question.
  • A long project where the source set builds across weeks instead of being fixed on day one.
  • K to 8: the teacher owns the folder and the class works from the projected notebook.
JulyGemini Notebook

NotebookLM is now called Gemini Notebook

A rename. Nothing moved. It is here because it is what people notice first.

Only the name changed. It is on this list because it is the change most likely to make someone think their tool disappeared, and because a rename is a free excuse to reintroduce something.

For you as a coach

Your own work

  • Update your own slides and handouts now, or spend three months correcting it in person.
  • Use the rename as the reason to go back to teachers who tried it eighteen months ago and moved on. They have not seen quizzes, flashcards, infographics or copying.

For the teacher

Design, develop, manage

  • Anything handed out last year that says NotebookLM now says the wrong thing. Worth a pass.
  • A teacher who abandoned it when it only summarized should be shown what it does now.

With students

Grades 9 to 12 direct

  • Nothing here, honestly. Fix the name on your materials and move on.

AugustGoogle Sheets

Combo charts, 3D bar import, and Excel pivot tables

Two measures on one chart, and Excel files that stop needing a rebuild.

For you as a coach

Your own work

  • A combo chart is what you want when a data meeting needs participation and outcome in one picture.
  • If an office keeps something important in Excel and you have been rebuilding it every time, stop.

For the teacher

Design, develop, manage

  • Assessment data arriving as Excel from a vendor now lands in Sheets with its pivot tables intact.

With students

All grades

  • Students comparing two different measures in a science or social studies data set finally get one chart instead of two.
AugustGoogle Calendar

Block invitations, and share availability without the details

Useful if your calendar is a shared punching bag.

For you as a coach

Your own work

  • Block invitations from anyone who keeps dropping meetings onto your coaching blocks.
  • Share your calendar so people see you are busy without seeing which teacher you are with.

For the teacher

Design, develop, manage

  • A teacher can publish planning availability without exposing everything else on their calendar.

With students

All grades

  • Not a student tool.

All grades
