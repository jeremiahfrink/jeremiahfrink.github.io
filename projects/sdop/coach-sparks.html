Idea sparks for coaches

Two halves. Coaching moves, which are about the craft and mostly about what you ask rather than what you open. And technology for your own work, which is about the hours around the coaching. Nothing here is a classroom activity. Those live on the session pages.

Two rules before you paste anything real

  1. The public test. Would you be comfortable with this becoming public? If not, take out the names and identifying details first. That applies to students, to teachers, and to anything evaluative.
  2. Every response is a starting point. Check what is missing, wrong, or unsupported before you act on it or hand it to anyone.

Four kinds of help. Filter if you are after one in particular.

Coaching moves

The craft. Most of these are about what you ask and what you decide, not about which tool is open. AI is the rehearsal partner, not the answer.

Deciding what to work on

Think better

Let it interview you first

The smallest move with the biggest payoff. Give it the questions before you give it the work.

Try this
I need to plan a session with a grade team. Do not draft anything yet. Ask me five questions about these teachers, what I have already tried with them, what success would look like, and what I cannot change. Summarize my direction back to me before you offer anything.
Coach better

Separate what you saw from what you concluded

Most observation notes mix evidence and judgment, and the judgment is what gets argued with.

Try this
Here are my notes from a classroom visit. Split them into two lists: things I actually observed, and things I concluded or assumed. For anything in the second list, tell me what evidence would be needed to support it and whether my notes contain that evidence.

Names out first. It works fine with "the teacher" and "a student."

Coach better

Find the one thing to say

You noticed nine things. Saying nine is the same as saying none.

Try this
Here is everything I noticed in a lesson. Rank them by which would most change student learning if it changed, not by which is easiest to fix or most visible. Tell me which one I should raise, which two can wait, and which are actually my preference rather than a problem.
Think better

Give it a process, not a problem

For the strategy everybody agreed to and nobody is doing.

Try this
Help me think through why a strategy is not sticking in these classrooms. Use a root cause logic tree. Ask me for evidence before you move from a possible cause to a recommendation, and tell me plainly when my evidence is thin.
Coach better

Plan the exit before you start the cycle

Name what this teacher looks like when they no longer need you.

Try this
I am starting a coaching cycle. Before we plan anything, help me describe what this teacher will be doing independently when the cycle is over, in observable terms. Then work backwards: what has to be true at the halfway point, and what would tell me I am creating dependence instead of capacity?

Conversations

Coach better

Prep the visit before you walk in

Find what you promised last time and never came back to.

Try this
Here are my notes from my last two visits with this teacher. Do not summarize them. List what I said I would follow up on, what I never came back to, what has changed between the visits, and the one question I should open with this time.
Coach better

Rehearse the follow-through conversation

The one with the teacher who agreed to try something and then did not.

Try this
Coach me through a conversation with someone who agreed to try something and did not do it. Ask me one question at a time about the outcome I want, what I actually know versus assume about why, and how this looks from their side. Do not give me a script. When I have answered, challenge my opening line and tell me how it is likely to land.
Think better

Ask for options and pushback, never the best plan

One recommendation is an answer. Two with costs is a conversation.

Try this
Give me two ways to approach this situation with a teacher. For each: what it prioritizes, what it costs, and where it is likely to break down. Then tell me what a skeptical twenty year veteran would say to both, and what I am missing.
Coach better

Model the thinking, not the tool

Use it in front of them, refuse the first answer, and say why out loud.

Try this
Work as my thought partner on this lesson. Do not give me a plan. Start by asking me what students need to be able to do and how I would know they got there. Push back if my answer is vague.

The output is not the point. The teacher watching you reject a plausible first answer is the point.

Coach better

Translate up

What you see across classrooms is information a principal does not have.

Try this
Here is a pattern I am seeing across several classrooms, described without naming anyone. Help me turn it into something a principal can act on: what the pattern is, what it is costing, what I am asking for, and what I am not asking for. Keep it under 200 words and take out anything that reads as evaluating individual teachers.

Checking your own thinking

Think better

Bring an advisory team to your own plan

Four reviewers who will each hate something different about it.

Try this
Review this plan as four different people: a twenty year veteran who has watched initiatives come and go, a second year teacher who is overwhelmed, a building principal, and a student. Have each raise their strongest objection. Where do they disagree with each other, and what must I fix before Thursday?
Think better

Pressure test it against a Tuesday in November

Almost everything works in a planning meeting in September.

Try this
Here is what I am about to recommend to a teacher. Stress test it against a real Tuesday in November: a fire drill, two absences, no prep period, and a teacher who is tired. What breaks first, what is the minimum version that still works, and what am I assuming about their time that is probably wrong?
Think better

Read your own session feedback honestly

The kind comments are not the data.

Try this
Here is the feedback from a session I ran. Find the themes, the outliers, and the tensions between what different people said. Separate what people said they enjoyed from what they said they would use. No recommendations yet. Then give me three questions I should answer before I plan the next one.
Coach better

Find out who you have not seen

Coaching time drifts toward the people who ask for it.

Try this
Here is how my last six weeks were spent, by teacher and by building. Which people have I not been in front of, where is my time actually concentrated, and what does that pattern suggest about who is getting support and who is not? Do not tell me what to do about it yet.
Coach better

Write the summary while it is still true

Otherwise you write it in June, from memory, and it is fiction.

Try this
Here are my notes across a coaching cycle. Draft a short summary: what we set out to work on, what actually changed in practice, what the evidence for that is, and what is still open. Keep my voice. Mark anything you inferred rather than found in the notes.

Technology for your own work

Nothing here helps a teacher directly. It gives you back the hours to go help one, or it cleans up work that was never the job in the first place.

Time back

Work smarter

Never start with a blank page

Hand over the mess. Organize first, interpret second.

Try this
Here are my rough notes. Step one: transcribe and organize them exactly as written. Do not summarize or interpret yet. Then, step two: organize them into themes and flag anything unclear, contradictory, or based on an assumption rather than something I recorded.
Work smarter

Make the calendar import file

Twenty visit dates typed once instead of twenty times.

Try this
Here is a list of dates, times, locations and titles. Produce a CSV I can import into Google Calendar using the exact column headers Google Calendar expects. Use my time zone. Do not add, merge or invent any event I did not list, and tell me if anything in my list is ambiguous.

Same move for a bulk list of PD sessions, observation windows, or a whole coaching cycle.

Work smarter

Clean up the spreadsheet someone sent you

Merged cells, three header rows, and a column of dates in four formats.

Try this
Here is some messy tabular data. Tell me what is wrong with it before you fix anything: inconsistent formats, duplicates, merged headers, missing values. Then give me a clean version with one header row and consistent formatting, and a short list of every change you made and anything you had to guess.
Work smarter

Get to the three lines you actually need

For the forty page policy document somebody forwarded with "thoughts?"

Try this
I need to know three things from this document: what it requires me to do, what it changes from before, and what it is silent on. Answer only those three, cite where in the document each answer comes from, and say so plainly if the document does not address one of them.
Work smarter

Constraints before possibilities

Especially if your week spans more than one building.

Try this
Before you build anything, make a constraint table for my coaching week: required, preferred, flexible, unknown. Flag any conflicts and label every assumption. Then turn each unknown into a question I need to answer before I schedule anything.
Work smarter

Turn a good prompt into a routine

The second time you do something well, write down how.

Try this
That worked. Turn what we just did into a repeatable process: name the steps, say where you should pause and ask me something, what criteria you use at each step, and what I decide rather than you. Write it so I could hand it to another coach.
Work smarter

Keep a decision log so you stop relitigating

Three months later nobody remembers why, so the argument runs again.

Try this
From these meeting notes, pull out every decision that was actually made. For each one give me: what was decided, who decided it, what the reasoning was, what was explicitly ruled out, and what was left open. Do not include anything that was only discussed.

Things you build once

Build better

Turn a walkthrough into look-fors

Make what you are noticing visible to the person being observed.

Try this
Help me build an informal look-fors checklist for classroom visits focused on the practice I am working on. Include what I would see students doing, what I would hear, and what would tell me it is not happening. One page. Add one reflection question I could ask in a debrief.
Build better

Package it so it runs without you

The test of a coaching resource is whether it survives your absence.

Try this
Write the guide for this: when to use it, what a teacher needs to prepare, the steps in order, what it will not do, and what still requires their own judgment. Write it for someone who was not in the room when I explained it.
Build better

Fix the survey before you send it

Most questions we write measure agreeableness.

Try this
Here are the questions I am about to send to teachers. For each one, tell me what it will actually measure rather than what I intend it to measure, whether it leads toward a particular answer, and whether I would know what to do differently based on any possible response. Rewrite the three weakest ones.
Build better

Draft the agenda for a meeting you are not running

You are often the only person who knows what has to get decided.

Try this
Here is the context for a meeting I am attending but not leading. List the decisions that need to be made, the questions likely to come up, whose voice is missing, and where people are likely to disagree. Then give me the two things I should say and the one thing I should not.
Build better

Build the case for your own time

Coaching gets cut by people who cannot see what it produced.

Try this
Help me make the case for protected coaching time to a building leader. Ask me what evidence I actually have before you write anything. Then draft the argument in their terms, not mine, and tell me the strongest counterargument and whether my evidence survives it.
Think better

Read the research article you will never finish

The one that has been open in a tab since August.

Try this
From this article, tell me: the claim, what evidence it rests on, how strong that evidence is, who it studied and whether that resembles our students, and what it does not claim that people will assume it does. Then give me one sentence I could say to a teacher about it.
