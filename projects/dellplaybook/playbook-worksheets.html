Email Templates (Appendix H)

Starting points, not scripts. Personalize the bracketed pieces and cut anything that does not fit the relationship.

Pre-event meeting outreach (two to four weeks ahead)

Subject: Connecting at [Conference Name]?

Hi [Name],

I saw that [District] will be at [Conference Name] on [dates], and I'll be there as well. I'd welcome 20 minutes with you while we're both in the building.

No agenda beyond your priorities: I'm particularly interested in how [specific initiative from their plan, board goals, or your last conversation] is progressing, and I may have a couple of things worth sharing from what we're seeing across the region on that front.

I'm holding time [day] between [window] and [day] between [window]. Would any of those work? Happy to meet at our space or wherever is convenient for you.

See you in [city],

[Your name]

Post-event follow-up (within one week)

Subject: Following up from [Conference Name]

Hi [Name],

Good to talk with you at [Conference Name]. I keep thinking about what you said regarding [specific thing they actually said; this line is the whole email].

As promised, here is [the resource, introduction, or answer you committed to]. [One sentence on why it connects to their situation.]

The natural next step on our side would be [specific, small next step: a call with X, a visit, a document]. Would [timeframe] work to pick this up?

[Your name]

The rule for both: one specific reference to them, one thing of value, one clear next step. If an email has none of those three, it is not ready to send.

Idea Catalogs

Ways People Have Shown Up (Appendix A)

A catalog of presence formats seen across education conferences, from Dell and others. Each entry: what it is, what it works for, and a watch-out. Strike what does not apply; add what you see in the field.

  • Interactive designed space. A larger footprint built as an experience rather than a booth: seating, demo stations, conversation areas, cohesive theming. Works for credibility and dwell time. Watch-out: cost scales fast; needs staffing depth to feel alive rather than empty.
  • Dedicated presentation room. A room within or adjacent to the exhibit space running scheduled sessions plus a device showcase, with unified lead capture (the ISTELive 26 model). Works for thought leadership and pipeline together. Watch-out: session promotion has to start weeks early or the room sits quiet.
  • Interactive classroom space. A working classroom environment used as an actual classroom during the event: real lessons, real furniture, real technology in use. Works for showing rather than telling; educators remember it. Watch-out: requires a teaching partner and serious logistics.
  • Esports / gaming lounge. A play space anchored on gaming hardware, often tied to esports programs and career pathways. Works for traffic, energy, and student-program conversations. Watch-out: traffic is not pipeline; pair it with a capture and conversation plan.
  • Student showcase. Students from a local district demonstrating their own work (robotics, media production, a signature program). Works for authenticity; nothing outdraws students presenting. Watch-out: coordinate early with the district, permissions included.
  • Co-sponsored single event. One well-executed moment co-funded with a partner: a reception, a breakfast, a session strand. Works for partner relationships and audience quality at moderate cost. Watch-out: agree on lead sharing and follow-up ownership up front.
  • One-on-one meeting lounge. A quiet, comfortable space dedicated to scheduled district conversations, sometimes with coffee service. Works for pipeline density. Watch-out: only as good as the booking discipline ahead of the event.
  • Hands-on lab / makerspace. Facilitated maker or coding activities attendees do themselves. Works for educator-heavy audiences and program credibility. Watch-out: facilitation is a full-time job; staff it separately from booth coverage.
  • Expert help desk. A "bring us your problem" desk staffed by strategists or engineers, no pitch. Works for credibility and surprising conversations. Watch-out: signal it clearly or it reads as just another booth counter.
  • Charging / recharge lounge. Seating, power, coffee. Works for dwell time and goodwill. Watch-out: pair with conversation starters or it is furniture with a logo.
  • Podcast or interview corner. Recording short interviews with district leaders on site, published afterward. Works for content factory value and gives leaders a reason to stop by. Watch-out: permissions and a realistic editing plan.
Creative Positioning Ideas (Appendix B)

A running list of beyond-the-booth concepts. Add to it after every event.

  • Annual sponsorship of the organizing body covering conference plus year-round programming
  • A sponsored session strand aligned to a theme (AI leadership, student programs, futures planning)
  • Invite-driven leadership dinner or breakfast adjacent to the conference
  • Pre-conference leadership session (the "Sunday session" model): a half-day for superintendents and CIOs/CTOs who need a reason to extend travel
  • Customer appreciation reception for existing accounts in the region
  • A hands-on student program showcase run with a local district already doing the program
  • "Ask the strategist" scheduled office hours within the booth or space
  • Co-hosted moment with an invested partner: joint session, joint reception, joint demo experience
  • District-to-district connection moments: a facilitated conversation among districts working the same problem, with Dell as convener rather than presenter
  • Charging station or quiet-space sponsorship done well (useful beats flashy)
  • Conference app or wifi sponsorship (visibility every attendee touches all day)
  • Awards or recognition segment sponsorship (celebrating educators positions Dell alongside the profession, not just the purchase)
  • Educator attendance scholarships: sponsoring registrations for teachers or leaders from under-resourced districts, in coordination with the organizer
  • Session recording sponsorship: funding the capture of conference sessions the organizer shares afterward, with Dell credited
  • Year-round webinar or workshop series co-built with the association, launched at the conference
  • Speaker or keynote dinner sponsorship (relationship access to the conference's featured voices)
  • Morning activity sponsorship (a fun run, a coffee walk) for informal relationship time
  • First-timer or new-leader orientation sponsorship: helping the organizer welcome new superintendents or CTOs, who are exactly the leaders building their vendor relationships fresh
