NYS & BOCES/RICs Explained

How purchasing and contracts actually work through BOCES and RICs in New York State, and the misconceptions that trip up deals.

What is a BOCES/RIC?

A BOCES (Board of Cooperative Education Services) provides a variety of services to school districts that are assigned to it (component school districts). These services have to be approved by the state education department to fit under the Cooperative Service (COSER) guidelines. These services can include the cooperative bidding and purchasing of technology hardware and software licensing. School districts purchase through the BOCES because they receive greater purchasing power, and because they receive aid for a percentage of what they purchase through the BOCES. There are 37 BOCES in the state (more here).

A RIC (Regional Information Center) is one of 12 specific BOCES across the state that are able to provide additional administrative technology services to both the other BOCES and the component districts (more detail here). The 12 RICs:

How does purchasing work with these BOCES/RICs?

School districts may purchase on their own or through their BOCES. All public entities must utilize an RFP or BID process when the annual purchases with a specific company will exceed the annual minimum set by the comptroller. This is why districts frequently purchase through their BOCES/RIC, with the BOCES or RIC holding the contract and the purchase.

Are there statewide contracts?

Yes. There are three primary types:

  • OGS. This is the contract that allows any public (and some non-public) organization to purchase through a bid process run by the NYS Office of General Services. Pretty much any public school and not-for-profit, non-public school can use this contract (full list here).
  • Multi-RIC contracts. These are coordinated by one RIC but will list the other RICs on it. Typically these are administrative technology licensing agreements or hardware bids. The Microsoft license agreement falls under this one.
  • Multi-BOCES contracts. These are RFP/BIDs done through one of the BOCES consortiums. Primary ones are Instructional Technology through Erie 1 BOCES, Distance Learning through Erie 1 BOCES, and School Library through Monroe 2 BOCES. Again, the RFP/BID and contract should list the participating BOCES.

So how do the purchases work then?

For OGS, this is a piggy-back contract that was written to allow any of the entities listed to utilize it and purchase from the vendor.

For BOCES/RICs, there is a lead BOCES/RIC that develops the contract specifics, and then the other BOCES/RICs may participate through that contract. Districts would then purchase through their assigned RIC/BOCES.

The customer from Dell's perspective is the BOCES/RIC listed in the contract, NOT the school district. We can't sell directly to the school district through these contracts; the PO comes from the BOCES/RIC.

What about the "Big 5"?

There are 5 large school districts in NYS that do not participate under a BOCES/RIC. In special circumstances they can purchase through one of those organizations through a cross-contract. The Big 5 are: Buffalo, Rochester, Syracuse, Yonkers, and New York City.

Common misconceptions

Open each one. If you've heard it in the field, you're not alone; that's why it's here.

"The RIC/BOCES contracts just allow anyone in the state to purchase with that set pricing."
No

These contracts are BOCES- or RIC-specific per the RFP/BID and only for those districts who participate in that service of the RIC/BOCES. POs must come from the RIC/BOCES, as they are the contract customer, not directly from the school district.

"School districts have to buy directly when they're using state or federal aid/grants so they're not 'double dipping.'"
No

To participate in the contract, they still need to go through the BOCES/RIC. However, they must designate that purchase as a "non-aidable" purchase due to the funding source, which will keep them from double-dipping.

"As long as one RIC or BOCES has a contract, any school district can purchase through them."
No

The school district has to belong to a service for a specific BOCES/RIC, and that BOCES/RIC must participate in the specific contract.
