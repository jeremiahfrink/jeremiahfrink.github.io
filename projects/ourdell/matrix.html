Reading this table for a first meeting

  • Where the state has done the work for you: Maryland (AI mandate, cybersecurity certification, phone law, all dated) and New York (Ed Law 2-d). Compliance is the door. Add Connecticut on privacy, where a non-conforming contract is simply void and only 8% of tracked products comply.
  • Where the state has left a vacuum: Pennsylvania and New Jersey on AI, Pennsylvania on privacy and cybersecurity. Add New Hampshire, which has published no AI guidance at all, and Vermont and Maine, which published guidance and funded none of it. Helping districts think is the door.
  • Where the regional layer is the route to market: New York first, then Pennsylvania, New Jersey, and Ohio. Vermont is now the sharpest case in the set, for a different reason: the supervisory union is not a channel but the operating layer, so selling around it is a category error. Where the layer is not the route: Virginia, Massachusetts for technology, Maryland (MEEC substitutes), Connecticut (services strong, purchasing weak), and Rhode Island, New Hampshire, and Maine, which have no regional layer at all.
  • Where the money conversation happens: a county building in Virginia and Maryland, a board finance committee in Pennsylvania, a county office in New Jersey, a ballot in New York and Ohio, a town meeting in Massachusetts. In Batch 3 it happens in a room full of voters: Town Meeting Day in Vermont, a March meeting or official ballot in New Hampshire, a budget meeting plus a validation referendum in Maine, and a charter-dependent mix of council, town meeting, and referendum in Connecticut and Rhode Island.
  • Where the calendar moves earliest: northern New England. Vermont votes the first Tuesday of March, New Hampshire in March with deliberative sessions in February, and Maine adopts in late May or June and validates by referendum within 45 days. A proposal that has not reached a board by roughly October or November has missed the year. That is about two months earlier than the mid-Atlantic states in Batches 1 and 2.
  • Where an out-of-state body holds the rail: a single Massachusetts cooperative now operates the student data privacy alliance for both New Hampshire and Rhode Island, which is worth knowing before treating either as a self-contained market.
