Closing the pool

Three things have to be ordered before the pool gets closed: the cord for the cover, the cover itself, and the chemicals. Each one needs a different measurement, and each one you have to work out yourself. Calculator is fine. Use 3.14 for pi.

What we measured
  • The pool is 18 feet across, edge to edge, straight through the middle.
  • The water is 4 feet deep.
  • The cover has to reach 1 foot past the edge, all the way around, or it slides in.

1. The cord

A cord runs around the outside edge of the cover and cinches underneath the rail. Order it too short and the cover comes off in the first storm. You need the distance around the cover.

How far across is the cover, edge to edge?

The pool is 18 feet across. The cover hangs 1 foot past the edge. Read that sentence twice before you answer.

feet

How long does the cord need to be to go all the way around the cover?

Distance around = pi × distance across

The distance around a circle is called its circumference. Round to one decimal place.

feet

2. The cover and the air pillows

Under the cover go air pillows. They sit on the water so ice pushes in on them instead of out on the pool wall, and they keep rain and snow running off to the sides instead of pooling in the middle. How many you need depends on how much cover there is to hold up, so you need the area of the cover. Area needs a different measurement than circumference did.

The area formula does not use the distance across. It uses the distance from the center to the edge. What is that measurement for the cover?

Center to edge is the radius. Edge to edge through the center is the diameter.

feet

How much material is in the cover? Find its area.

Area = pi × radius × radius

Square the radius first, then multiply by 3.14. Round to the nearest whole number.

square feet

Our rule: one 4 foot by 4 foot air pillow for every 250 square feet of cover. How many pillows do we buy?

Divide, then think about what the answer means. You are ordering a physical object.

pillows

3. The chemicals

Closing chemicals are dosed by the gallon. Too little and the water turns green under the cover by March. Too much and you have wasted it. So you need to know how much water is actually in the pool, which means volume, not area.

A round pool is a cylinder. Find the area of the circle on the bottom, then push it down through the depth.

Back to the pool itself now, not the cover. What is the pool's radius?

feet

Square that radius.

Squaring means multiplying a number by itself.

square feet

Finish the area of the pool floor. Multiply by pi.

Area = pi × radius × radius

Round to two decimal places.

square feet

Now push that floor down through the water. Multiply the floor area by the depth.

Volume = area of the floor × depth

Round to the nearest whole number.

cubic feet

Last one. The chemical label is written in gallons. Convert.

Gallons = cubic feet × 7.48

One cubic foot of water is 7.48 gallons. Round to the nearest whole gallon.

gallons
