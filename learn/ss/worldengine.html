The World Engine

You wrote a world. Now make it one you could actually run: a place where a bad year, a greedy neighbor, or a dead king does something, and the rules decide what.

Day 1 Day 2 Day 3

How this works

Your Portfolio is the raw material. This page walks through it one part at a time. In each part you paste what you already wrote, then answer the questions that turn it from description into a system. On Day 3 you distill the whole thing into a twelve clause Charter, and then your world gets played against it.

Everything you type here saves in this browser automatically. When you are done, use the buttons at the top to copy or download it as Markdown, then paste it into a Google Doc to turn it in.

  • Answer from your world, not from what would be cool. If your Culture Profile says the people are proud and independent, then your answer about who does the unwanted work has to fit proud, independent people.
  • Earth rules in the founding region. Water, food, defense, and growth work there the way they work here. Magic, if it exists there, costs something and someone controls who gets to use it.
  • Silent is an answer. If your Portfolio never decided something, write "silent" and then decide it now. Keep the word "silent" in your answer so the gap stays visible.
  • Nobody is grading whether it is historically right. You are being graded on whether the parts agree with each other and whether you committed.

Day 3: The Charter, then Play

About 4 hours. Morning is the Charter and the comparison. Afternoon your world gets played.

Part 7: The Charter

Read Parts 1 through 6 back to front before you start. The Charter is what all of it boils down to.

On paper first. One heavy sheet, handwritten in your italic hand. No more than twelve numbered clauses. Every one of the Five Decisions has to be traceable to at least one clause. Name the society. Date it. Sign it. Twelve is the limit, not the target. Then type the clauses below so they export with the rest, and photograph the sheet.

From this moment on, nothing on the sheet is erased. Changes are dated amendments below the clauses. A struck clause gets one line through it and stays readable.

The comparison

Your Charter goes next to a real one, nearly 4,000 years old. You will be given the extract.

Part 8: Play Your World

Five situations, one at a time, read to you. Ten minutes each, out loud, recorder on, no research, no notes. You answer from the Charter as written. Not the Charter you wish you had written. If it cannot handle the situation, you amend it on the spot, dated, in your hand. Then you log it here. The first card is practice and you get the answer right away. The other four you do not; their answers come from history later this year.
