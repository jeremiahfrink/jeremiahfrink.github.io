What you are doing

Every society is a set of answers to a few questions nobody gets to skip: where does the food come from, who decides, who does the work nobody wants, what happens when someone breaks a rule, and what gets written down. On Day 1 you answer those out loud, fast, knowing nothing. Then you spend eight days building a world with the Oneschool course. On Day 9 you write that world's founding document. On Day 10 you find out what it cannot handle.

You will get things wrong. That is the point. Everything you make in these ten days stays on the record, and the rest of the year is history arguing with you about it.

  • Build the world first. Write the laws after. The Charter comes on Day 9, once you know what you built. Do not write rules early.
  • Nobody is checking whether you are historically right. You have not been taught any history yet. You are being checked on whether you commit, and whether your world agrees with itself.
  • Any genre you want. Fantasy, science fiction, something nobody has named. One limit: the region where your founding society lives runs on Earth rules for water, food, defense, and growth. If magic exists there, it costs something and somebody controls who gets to use it.
  • Say it before you write it. A lot of the thinking happens out loud with the recorder running.
  • From Day 9 on, nothing gets erased. Changes to the Charter are dated amendments in your handwriting. Struck clauses get one line through them and stay readable.

1Tue Sept 15

The Drop

About 3.5 hours. Morning on paper and out loud. Afternoon is Lesson 1.

You get a map with no names, no borders, no scale. Somewhere on it, you have to put people.

Pick a site and defend it

Mark where you would settle. Recorder on. Explain why. You will be pushed on water, food, defense, and what happens when there are three times as many of you. Then you get a second map. Decide again.

The Five Decisions

Out loud, timed, no looking anything up. Answer fast. This recording gets filed and you do not hear it again until Day 9.

  • Where does water and food come from, and what happens in a bad year?
  • Who makes decisions, and how does the next person get the job?
  • Who does the work nobody wants, and why do they keep doing it?
  • What happens to someone who breaks a rule, and who decides that?
  • What gets written down, who can read it, and who keeps the copies?

The sealed verdict

One question, five minutes, recorder on, no preparation. Then the recording is sealed and you will not hear it again until June. Nobody grades this. It only has to exist.

Afternoon: Lesson 1, The Spark That Births a World

Watch the video. Read the worksheet. Open your Lesson 1 document.

Build the World Vision Page, with two changes

  • Step 3 The main conflict or tension has to be one of the Five Decisions going wrong. Not a villain. A decision.
  • Step 5 The society you founded this morning is named on the page as the founding people of this world. Other peoples can exist. This one is yours.

2Wed Sept 16

The Land

About 3 hours. Oneschool Lesson 2, Geography and Maps.

Watch and read

Lesson 2 video and worksheet. Short answers go in the Lesson 2 document. The map itself is on paper.

Draw the map, with four additions

Hand drawn on the heavy sheet, labels in your italic hand. Three regions, two or three named places, a river or coast, a compass rose, a title. Plus:

  • Your settlement from Day 1 goes on the map, with its river drawn from source to sea and the floodplain marked. A note in the margin says why this spot works.
  • Mark a second site you considered and rejected. Write the reason next to it.
  • Put a symbol for the storehouse in a place you could defend. Note who guards it.
  • Color the climate zones. The zone your founding society lives in has to match what you said about food on Day 1.

Close

Map in the folder. Lesson 2 document saved.

Watch and read

Lesson 3 video and worksheet. Lesson 3 document open.

Build the Culture Profile, with three changes

The profile is for your founding society. The five words, five sayings, and the greeting are handwritten.

  • Step 6 One of your five sayings has to carry a rule of your society inside it, the way the worksheet's Quechua example carries three laws in nine words.
  • Step 8 "What they value most" has to explain something specific: why people do the work nobody wants without being forced.
  • Step 8 "How they connect or conflict with others" names one specific neighboring people and says exactly what the friction is about. You will need this neighbor later.

Food

Watch and read

Lesson 6 video and worksheet. Lesson 6 document open. Map on the desk.

Build one creature entry and one plant entry, with three changes

7Wed Sept 23

Memory

About 3.5 hours. Oneschool Lesson 7, History, Legends, and Myths. The hardest day of the course.

Watch and read

Lesson 7 video and worksheet. Pay attention to the two excerpts in Step 2, the Ministry version and the elder's version. You are about to write both.

Build the Chronicle of Ages twice, and a mostly empty timeline

  • Step 6 First version: the official history, in the voice of your society's ruler or record keeper. It is a ruler explaining why he deserves to rule.
  • Step 6 Second version: the same events, told by the person your society is hardest on, the one doing the work nobody wants. Told years later. Written down by someone else.
  • Step 5 The timeline gets two eras named: the founding and now. Leave the rest blank. Every crisis this year will fill one in.
  • Step 1 "Which stories are silenced": name one specific rule or event the ruler's version leaves out, and say why.

8Thu Sept 24

The Portfolio

About 3 hours. Oneschool Lesson 8, Bringing It All Together.

Watch and read

Lesson 8 video and worksheet.

Run the consistency check with everything open

Map, Culture Profile, Codex of Power, Tech Codex, species entries, both Chronicles. Go through the Step 2 table. Every "no" gets fixed or gets written down as a known problem you are choosing to leave. You are allowed to leave things broken. You are not allowed to hide them.

Assemble the Portfolio

Title page with the world's name and a short description. A crest or motto. The seven sections from the worksheet, plus one more:

  • Section 8: Silences and Amendments. For now it holds only your list of known problems. It fills up on Day 10 and keeps filling all year.

Present the world

Fifteen minutes, recorder on. Walk someone through your world as if they have never heard of it. Start wherever you think the world starts.

Then five minutes on the worksheet's last question: what did you learn about yourself as a creator? That recording gets sealed next to the one from Day 1. Both stay closed until June.

Close

Portfolio saved. Recordings filed as Day 8.

9Fri Sept 25

The Charter

About 3.5 hours. No video. Today you write the founding document of the society inside your world.

Playback

Listen to your Five Decisions recording from Day 1 for the first time since you made it. Recorder on. Say what your world kept, what it dropped, and what it reversed.

Write the Charter

One heavy sheet. Handwritten in your italic hand. No more than twelve numbered clauses. Every one of the Five Decisions has to be traceable to at least one clause. Every clause has to agree with the Portfolio, or you write the disagreement into Section 8. Name the society. Date it. Sign it.

Twelve is the limit, not the target. Brevity is the hard part.

From this moment on, nothing on the sheet is erased. Ever.

The comparison

Your Charter goes next to a real one, nearly 4,000 years old. You run five questions on the old one: who made this, when, for whom, why, and what is it trying to make you believe. Then one question about yourself: what problem did they anticipate that you did not? Three typed sentences.

Open the Pattern Codex

New typed document. Write three predictions, not facts. Each one takes the form: societies that do X will tend to end up with Y, because of Z. Draw them from your Charter and your Portfolio. Each prediction gets these fields, every time, all year:

  • The prediction, with a short name
  • The mechanism, in your own words
  • Cases: leave empty for now
  • Current event sightings: leave empty
  • World engine note: how this would work as a game system

Predictions get confirmed, complicated, or destroyed as the year goes. A prediction that survives untouched should worry you, not please you.

Close

Charter in the folder next to the Map. Codex saved. Recordings filed as Day 9. Weekend.

10Mon Sept 28

Play Your World

About 3 hours. Five cards. Charter on the desk. Pen ready.

Each card is a situation your society has to deal with. You get ten minutes, out loud, no research, no notes. You answer from the Charter as written. Not the Charter you wish you had written. If the Charter cannot handle it, you amend it on the spot, dated, in your hand.

How each card runs

  • The situation is read to you once.
  • Ten minutes, recorder on. You will be pressed with three questions. Nobody will tell you if you are right.
  • Ten minutes typed: one new Codex prediction the card exposed, and one line in Section 8 saying what changed in the Charter and why. If nothing changed, write why it held.

Five cards, one at a time

The first is a practice card and you get the answer right away. The other four you do not. Their answers come later in the year, from history, one unit at a time.

Close

Charter carrying its first amendments. Section 8 holding your first revision. Codex holding your first real predictions. Portfolio and Charter handed to Capstone. Recordings filed as Day 10.

Tomorrow a card gets issued from a different deck. You will not see that one coming either. Ten months of that.

After the ten days

Your world is finished for now, and it is about to start losing arguments. Each unit this year opens with a crisis card and closes with the answer history already found. Every card that forces a change goes into the Charter as a dated amendment and onto the timeline as a named era.

In June you rebuild the whole thing knowing what you know then, and the two sealed recordings get opened. The rebuilt world is not the final product. The difference between it and this one is.
